# How to Get an A

I’m a sixth grader, and I got curious about data discovery by accident. My dad introduced me to the course, I just gave a try. I did not expect to work on a science project about data, but once I started, I was intrigued. I wondered what a bunch of numbers could tell me, I wanted to find out.

My teammate Sophie , an 8th grader, and I picked a question that felt close to home: **How do you get an A?** We’re both busy, and sometimes it’s hard to decide what to do with the time we have before a test. Should we spend a little more time reviewing, put our screens away earlier, or just go to sleep? We wanted to see what we could learn from the data and come away with ideas we could try ourselves.

### How we worked through the project

1. **We started with a guess.** We thought getting more sleep, staying awake in class, and looking over the material before a test might go along with better scores.
2. **We got our data.** Dr. Su gave us a dataset of 500 simulated students. It included their test scores, sleep, screen time, homework time, and whether they fell asleep in class or looked over the material before the test.
3. **We cleaned the data and made graphs.** We used Python in Google Colab to remove rows with missing data and plot sleep and screen time against test scores. Seeing dots on a graph made it easier for me to spot patterns than looking at a table full of numbers.
4. **We looked at who scored 82.5, not 90, or higher.** In our simulated dataset, everyone who scored at least 90 slept at least seven hours and had less than three hours of screen time. Most of those students slept at least eight hours.
5. **We put our findings into slides.** The patterns gave us ideas to try in our own lives, like getting enough sleep and looking over the material ahead of a test. I’d like to see what happens when I try those habits myself.

My favorite part was taking a question I actually cared about and using graphs to see what I could find out. Thank to Professor Huang for helping me get started with data discovery and making it fun to learn!
